# Adversarial review log

The pipeline was reviewed in rounds by a reviewer playing a skeptical senior data engineer on an AWS Glue/Athena team, who was looking for reasons *not* to hire the author. The reviewer worked from a clean checkout with no access to the author's notes, ran everything, and hand-built hostile inputs. Each finding is recorded with its verdict and what changed. Every fix that changed behaviour has a regression test.

## Round 1

**Reviewer's verdict:** lean no. The biggest reason was that three headline claims ("incremental equals full", "quarantine instead of drop", reconciliation catching mis-paid receipts) broke on small hand-made inputs outside the generator's defect catalog, and that the 100% scorecard largely graded the pipeline against its own generator.

There is no "touched months" logic left to get wrong. | `test_correction_that_moves_a_record_to_another_month` |
| 2 | Blocker | Rejected records from a file whose lines are back-dated into another month reached neither silver nor quarantine. | Confirmed | Same fix as #1, which never uses record months to decide quarantine writes. | `test_rejects_from_back_dated_lines_reach_quarantine` |

**Also found while fixing:** the stricter, outcome-based evaluator first flagged one `duplicate_row` and one `corrected_resend` as unhandled. The cause was an evaluator bug: it looked up the cell by the file's date instead of the event's date, because the manifest did not carry the record's own business date. The manifest now does.

## Round 2

**Reviewer's verdict:** do not advance yet. The biggest reason was that reconciliation could mark a variance of any size as "explained by quarantine": one junk record on the right day was enough, and the evaluator only checked two of the seven checks for masking. The round-1 fixes held up under variations (incremental equals full across 28 random batch splits, strict parsing, month moves, failure isolation).

| # | Severity | Finding | Verdict | What changed | Test |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Blocker | An unsized related quarantined record made any variance "explained". The `>12` candidate shortcut also explained everything. Quarantine was dated by its file, and a record that was later corrected and loaded still explained variances. | Confirmed | **New status `needs_review`:** an unsized record can no longer explain anything. Effects carry a direction (`+?`/`-?`), and a review needs the direction to fit and the variance to be within any bound on the unknown size (several lost MES rows are bounded by the station's issued-minus-produced gap). More than 12 candidates is review-or-exception, never explained. Quarantined records are dated by their own date and released once their key loads. Bank-line deposits and payments are recovered as sizes. | `test_unsized_quarantine_never_explains_and_is_bounded`, `test_unsized_quarantine_must_point_the_right_way`, `test_unknown_key_quarantine_explains_only_an_exact_match`, `test_quarantine_is_released_when_its_key_loads_later`, `test_quarantined_record_is_dated_by_its_own_date`, `test_quarantined_invoice_amount_explains_a_deposit_gap_exactly` |
| 2 | Major | The scorecard was largely self-graded: 91% of "handled" was code mapping; only one kind of business error was injected; four checks were matched by construction; `late_file` claimed later batches but the scorecard ran one batch; some checks only confirmed a mechanism ran. | Confirmed | Code variants are reported in their own table.
